Where Auditors Look — A Plain-English Map for the Developer

v0.1 — 6 July 2026. Every area an approved quality auditor examines, what they open, and which Rise documents answer — with the citation for each. Written for the developer: these are the retrieval patterns the platform must serve. Sources: the Quality Indicators Guidelines (compilation C03, in force 1 Jul 2026 — section numbers below are that instrument's, verified against its table of contents on 6 Jul 2026), the PRPS Rules 2018, the Commission's audit pages, and — where marked — practitioner consensus rather than an instrument.

One design implication before the list: every query below is "standard → documents → records." That is the graph's IMPLEMENTS edge walked backwards plus RECORDS_TO walked forwards. If those two traversals are fast, the platform answers an audit.

1. How the audit runs (the frame)

Certification (higher-risk supports): a document review, then site visits with interviews of workers and participants — participant sampling is opt-out, so participants are included unless they decline (Commission auditor guidance). Reports go to the Commission within 28 days of completion; verification audits (lower-risk, desktop) within 14 days; a smaller mid-term audit sits inside the registration cycle with a governance focus (Commission, types-of-audits page). The auditor recommends; the Commission decides. Sampling methodology sits in the AQA Scheme Guidelines (F2018N00114), Annex B.

The auditor's opening move in every area, per practitioner consensus: open the register first, then trace sampled rows back to completed forms and forward to actions. Prose is checked last.

2. The areas, one by one

Format: Area (QI section) — what they open → Rise documents.

Rights of participants (QI ss 6–10)

Person-centred practice, values and beliefs, privacy and dignity, informed choice and supported decision-making, and freedom from violence, abuse, neglect, exploitation and discrimination. They open: participant files, consent records, the participant handbook, interview participants directly. → POL-RGT-01/02/03, POL-SGD-01, FRM-RGT-01, FRM-INF-01, STA-RGT-01, PRO-SGD-01.

Governance and operational management (QI ss 11, 11A)

Who is accountable, whether leadership actually oversees quality and safety. They open: delegations, board/management minutes showing compliance on the agenda, conflict-of-interest declarations and register, notification history to the Commission. → POL-GOV-01/02/03, PRO-GOV-01/02, REG-GOV-01/02, FRM-GOV-01, and this whole register (REG-GOV-03) as evidence of document control. Mid-term audits concentrate here.

Risk management (QI s 12; verification pathway s 77)

They open: the risk register — currency, ownership, participant-specific risks flowing into support plans, incidents feeding back in. → POL-RSK-01, PRO-RSK-01, FRM-RSK-01, REG-RSK-01.

Quality management (QI s 13)

They open: the improvement register, internal audit schedule and results, version control across sampled documents (they check footer dates). → POL-QMS-01, PRO-QMS-01/02/03, REG-QMS-01/02, FRM-QMS-01.

Information management (QI s 14)

They open: consent records, access controls, breach log, retention practice. → POL-INF-01, PRO-INF-01/02, FRM-INF-01, REG-INF-01.

Feedback and complaints (QI s 15; verification s 76; Complaints Rules F2018L00634)

They open: the complaints register first — then trace individual complaints: acknowledged? procedurally fair? complainant told about the Commission? improvement logged? They ask participants whether they know how to complain. → POL-FBK-01, PRO-FBK-01, WIN-FBK-01, FRM-FBK-01, REG-FBK-01.

Incident management (QI s 16; verification s 75; Incident Rules F2018L00633)

The most-sampled register in the suite (practitioner consensus). They trace: incident → form completeness → register entry timing → reportability assessment → Commission notification within the statutory window → corrective action → participant follow-up. → POL-INC-01, PRO-INC-01/02, WIN-INC-01, FRM-INC-01, REG-INC-01. The whole chain is fully drafted in the suite as the worked example.

Human resource management (QI s 17; verification s 74; Worker Screening Rules F2018L00887)

They open: worker files — screening checks verified before start dates (they compare dates), Code of Conduct acknowledgements, qualifications, induction, supervision, training matrix currency. → POL-HRM-01, POL-COC-01, PRO-HRM-01/02/03, FRM-HRM-01/02, REG-HRM-01/02.

Continuity, emergency and disaster (QI ss 18, 18A)

They open: the business continuity plan, emergency plans per setting and per participant need, evidence of drills. A plan with no test record reads as a document, not a capability. → POL-COS-01, PLN-COS-01, PRO-COS-01, POL-EDM-01, PLN-EDM-01, PRO-EDM-01, REG-EDM-01.

Provision of supports (QI ss 19–23)

The participant-file walk: intake and access decision → support plan (current, participant-led) → service agreement (signed, plain language, copy provided) → responsiveness evidence → planned transitions. They sample whole files end to end. → POL-SUP-01…04, PRO-SUP-01…04, FRM-SUP-01…04, AGR-SUP-01, REG-SUP-01.

Support provision environment (QI ss 24–27, incl. 26A mealtime)

They walk the site: safety checklists done and hazards closed out; participant money records reconciled to receipts; medication charts complete with competency evidence behind each administering worker; mealtime plans followed as written; waste and infection control. → POL-ENV-01, POL-MNY-01, POL-MED-01, POL-MTM-01, POL-WST-01 and their procedures, forms and registers; POL-WHS-01 alongside.

Module 1 — high intensity supports (QI ss 29–35, incl. 30A dysphagia)

The sharpest evidence test in the standards: for each HIDPA skill delivered, workers trained and assessed by an appropriately qualified health practitioner, with current competency records, plus a health support plan per participant. They match rosters to the competency register. → POL-HID-01, PRO-HID-01…08, FRM-HID-01, REG-HID-01, REG-HRM-02.

Modules 2 / 2A — behaviour support (QI ss 38–44 specialist; ss 47–54 implementing; RP Rules F2018L00632)

They open: behaviour support plans (current, authorised), the restrictive practices register, every-use records, and evidence of monthly online reporting to the Commission — an explicit indicator (QI s 51 area). Unauthorised use traced into the reportable-incident pathway. → POL-BSP-01, PRO-BSP-01/02/03, FRM-BSP-01, REG-BSP-01.

Module 3 — early childhood (QI ss 56–62)

Family-centred practice in the files: family goals, key worker records, natural settings, outcome measurement. → POL-ECS-01, PRO-ECS-01.

Module 4 — support coordination (QI ss 64–66)

Conflict of interest is the centre of gravity: they look for documented options given to participants and declared conflicts wherever the provider coordinates toward its own services. → POL-SCO-01, PRO-SCO-01, REG-GOV-02.

Module 5 — SDA (QI ss 68–72)

Dwelling enrolment records, tenancy management, SDA-specific agreements and conflict handling. → POL-SDA-01, PRO-SDA-01, REG-SDA-01.

Module 5A — SIL (QI ss 72B–72E; new, from 1 Jul 2026)

House-level evidence, not just organisation-level: decision-support records (72B), home safeguarding with dignity of risk (72C), practice governance per house — supervision, rosters, handovers, house-level incident visibility (72D), and agreements about tenancy, housing and support arrangements, with Easy Read (72E). → POL-RGT-03 + PRO-SIL-01 + FRM-SIL-01; POL-SIL-02 + PRO-SIL-02; POL-SIL-03 + PRO-SIL-03 + REG-SIL-01; PRO-SIL-04 + AGR-SIL-01. Expect auditors to be calibrating here too — the module is weeks old.

Verification pathway (QI ss 74–77)

Lower-risk providers, desktop only, four areas: HR, incidents, complaints, risk. The register rows tagged "Verification" in Applies-to are exactly this pack.

3. Cross-cutting behaviours to design for

All practitioner-consensus unless cited: registers before prose; dates compared (screening vs start, incident vs entry, notification vs deadline); version footers checked against the master register; workers asked to describe a procedure and the answer compared to the document; participants interviewed with opt-out sampling (Commission, verified); the evidence trail walked in both directions. Each of these is a graph query the platform should make one click.

4. What this map is not

Not the indicators themselves — their text lives in the QI Guidelines (F2018N00041, C03) and is deliberately pointered, not copied, in Rise. Not legal advice. And not a guarantee of auditor behaviour: instruments set what must be assessed; the sequencing and emphasis above is how experienced practitioners report it plays out.
