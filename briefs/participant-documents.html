The Participant
Documents Brief

Vanna, with April
30 September 2026
Brief № 3

What a participant's document checklist should hold, where each item comes from, what stays off it and why, and how it mirrors the workforce page. Written from CPG's own Stage 2 file list, the Harmony Care audit plan and the 1 July 2026 Quality Indicators, cross-checked against real audit reports.

01Decision

Build it, on the workforce pattern

Decision. The participants page gets a required-documents checklist, the same pattern as the workforce page. A certification audit has two stages, and Stage 2 is mostly the auditor reading a sample of participant files, at least five participants under opt-out sampling. So this checklist is audit evidence, the twin of the workforce checklist, not participant management.

Scope line. Fifteen catalogue items, a required set per participant, filed documents matched against it. Nothing that drifts into a participant CRM: no shifts, progress notes, budgets or rosters. That is a different product and it is where the time would go.

The CPG guide to certification audits, the same auditor Harmony Care used, says it plainly: Stage 1 checks policies, procedures and templates, Stage 2 checks staff and participant files. The auditor selects the participant sample about a week before, then asks for the selected files in a shared folder or through the provider's client software.

The repo already holds participants and their documents, and the auditor view-as-provider surface reads them. What is missing is the required set and the match between filed and required, which is exactly what the workforce page has.

Where the list comes from

  • CPG, Guide for Your NDIS Certification Audit, Iss1 Rev3, Stage 2 “Participant Files”, eighteen numbered items.
  • CPG, Harmony Care provisional audit plan, 18 and 19 August 2026, “Participant files” and “Risk assessment templates”.
  • NDIS Commission, Quality Indicators Guidelines 2018, compilation 3, 1 July 2026, the clause behind each item.

Real reports back it. Catalyst Training (SAI Global, 2022): every file held consents re-signed within twelve months. Afford (SAI Global, 2020): management plans sighted in every file for a participant with a medical condition.

02The checklist

Nine core, six conditional

Bucket: participant. Fields follow the expected_documents catalogue: title, provenance, expiry, clause. Core items apply to every participant and are ticked by default. Conditional items depend on the supports delivered, like the workforce page's per-role toggles.

Mandated an instrument or verification list names it. Expected a Core Module outcome or quality indicator calls for it. Convention audit practice, no instrument requires it.

03Left off

What was left off, and why

CPG's Stage 2 list has eighteen items. Fifteen made the checklist. The other three, plus incidents and complaints which the auditor also reads in a participant's file, were left off for one reason: they are streams of records, not filed documents. A filed document is present or missing, and valid or expired. A record stream has neither state. Progress notes are never “valid until”. You cannot tick progress notes once and be done, and a green VALID pill on them would be a lie. The checklist model that works for the workforce page does not fit them.

The counter-argument is real. An auditor who opens a sampled file and finds no notes for the last month will raise it, whatever the checklist says. So these are not unimportant, they are a different shape. Each one below says what it is, why it is off, and where it belongs.

Case, progress, shift and therapy notes

CPG item 7. Written per shift or session, they show supports were delivered as the plan says and that changes were noticed.

Why off: a continuous stream with no expiry. Belongs in: a records surface with a recency signal, such as “last note 3 days ago”, or the provider's own client software, which the auditor is given access to.

Records of collaboration with other providers and practitioners

CPG item 8. GP letters, allied health correspondence, plan manager and support coordinator contact, referrals.

Why off: correspondence accumulates, it is not one document. Belongs in: the same records surface, or as attachments under the support plan. Module 3 correspondence (CPG item 18) is the same shape.

ABC charts and behaviour logs

CPG item 16. Antecedent, behaviour, consequence entries kept for a participant with a behaviour support plan, which feed the plan's review.

Why off: a log, not a plan. Belongs in: the records surface, tied to the behaviour support plan, which is on the checklist and does expire.

Incidents and complaints involving the participant

Not a CPG file item, but the auditor cross-checks the incident and complaints registers against sampled files.

Why off: they live in the registers, which are already business evidence in the catalogue. Belongs in: a per-participant view of the registers, when those registers exist in Rise.

How the participant signals an urgent health concern

CPG item 11, for participants with communication difficulties. CPG also asks the intake process to capture it.

Why off: a section of two documents already on the list, not a document itself. Belongs in: the intake form and the support plan.

The call that is open

If the checklist should say something about the streams, the honest version is a second group under the fifteen, labelled records, with a recency state instead of VALID. A small addition, but a second model on one page, so it is Jesse's decision, not a default.

04Build notes

What changes in the code

  • The participant_documents table carries a check constraint that allows only four categories. It has to go, replaced by a link to the catalogue code, the way the workforce page matches a file to an item.
  • Four items expire on a date rather than a cadence: the service agreement, the schedule of supports, the NDIS plan and the behaviour support plan. Capture an expiry date on upload, as a Working With Children Check already needs.
  • Default the nine core items ticked when a participant is created. Only the six conditional items need a human decision.
  • Consent gates the audit itself. The auditor may only receive a participant's file once that participant has consented, so the privacy consent item is the one to surface first when it is missing.
