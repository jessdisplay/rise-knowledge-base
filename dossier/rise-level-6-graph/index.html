Everything is a dot. Every connection is a labelled arrow.

A rule is a dot. Your policy is a dot. A register row is a dot. The arrow between two dots always says why they connect:

Your policyimplements →the rule
Your register rowevidences →the checklist

There are 25 kinds of arrow. Because the arrows are labelled, the machine can answer both directions of the only two questions that matter: why does this exist? and what depends on this?

The machine never forgets

Nothing is ever deleted. When something changes, the old arrow is kept and marked "replaced". That is how Rise can answer an auditor's favourite question: "show me how things stood on this date last year."

The machine remembers who said so

Every arrow records its source: a human made it, it was read straight out of the law, or a computer suggested it. Computer suggestions are always marked "needs review" until a person confirms them. Trust is recorded, never assumed.

Why the gaps you saw at Level 4 are possible

A gap is simply a dot that should have an arrow and doesn't. Because the arrows are labelled and dated, the machine can find every missing one — for your organisation, against your rules, today.
