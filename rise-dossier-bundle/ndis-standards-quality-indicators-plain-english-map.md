# Every NDIS Practice Standard mapped to its Quality Indicators — in plain English

**What this is.** The complete map of the NDIS Practice Standards to the quality
indicators auditors assess them against, with every pairing taken directly from the
law itself, and a one-line plain-English summary of what auditors look for under
each standard.

**Source.** The National Disability Insurance Scheme (Quality Indicators for NDIS
Practice Standards) Guidelines 2018, Compilation No. 1 (as in force 15 November
2021), fetched in full from the Federal Register of Legislation on 6 July 2026. The
Guidelines are made under subsection 181D(2) of the National Disability Insurance
Scheme Act 2013.

**How to read the references.**
- "Schedule 7, clause 4" points into the *Rules* — the National Disability Insurance
  Scheme (Provider Registration and Practice Standards) Rules 2018, where the
  standards themselves are printed. A schedule is an attachment at the back of a
  law; a clause is a numbered paragraph inside it.
- "Section 69" points into the *Guidelines* — where the matching indicators are
  printed.
- The pairings below are not my judgement. Each module of the Guidelines opens with
  a table that legislates exactly which section matches which clause. This document
  reproduces those eleven tables and adds plain-English names and summaries.

**The one rule underneath everything:** every standard is written as an *outcome*
(a sentence describing what good looks like) followed by *indicators* the law says
"should be demonstrated" — the things an auditor checks.

## Module 1 — high intensity daily personal activities

Complex hands-on care. Every standard here follows the same shape: a personal plan
developed with the person and reviewed by a qualified health practitioner, written
policies including a worker training plan, and workers trained to the official
skills descriptor for that specific support.

| The standard | In the Rules at | Indicators at | What auditors look for, in one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Complex bowel care | Schedule 2, clause 3 | section 29 | A practitioner-reviewed personal plan, written procedures, and workers trained to the official skills descriptor for this care. |
| Enteral (tube) feeding and management | Schedule 2, clause 4 | section 30 | The same plan-policies-training trio, specific to each person's feeding method and regime. |
| Severe dysphagia (swallowing difficulty) management | Schedule 2, clause 4A | section 30A | People needing it are identified, professionally assessed, and supported by workers trained for their specific plan and preferences. |
| Tracheostomy management | Schedule 2, clause 5 | section 31 | Plan, policies and descriptor-level training for tracheostomy care and suctioning. |
| Urinary catheter management | Schedule 2, clause 6 | section 32 | Plan, policies and descriptor-level training for each catheter type. |
| Ventilator management | Schedule 2, clause 7 | section 33 | Plan, policies and descriptor-level training specific to the ventilator used. |
| Subcutaneous injections | Schedule 2, clause 8 | section 34 | All of the above plus documented written or phone orders from the prescribing health practitioner. |
| Complex wound management | Schedule 2, clause 9 | section 35 | Plan, policies and descriptor-level training covering how the wound regime affects daily supports. |

## Things worth knowing about this map

1. **Count.** This compilation contains 66 standards with indicators (24 core, 8 in
   Module 1, 7 in Module 2, 8 in Module 2A, 7 in Module 3, 3 in Module 4, 5 in
   Module 5, 4 in Verification). A count of 70 — as shown in the Rise build — is
   consistent with the supported independent living standards added in 2026, which
   are **not** in this compilation and not verified here. That reconciliation is an
   inference, not a checked fact.
2. **Lettered numbers (16A, 18A, 26A, 30A) mean "inserted later."** Commonwealth
   drafting adds letters rather than renumbering everything when new standards are
   inserted — here, the 2021 amendments that added emergency management, mealtime
   management and severe dysphagia.
3. **Gaps in the clause numbers** (the core module jumps from clause 7 to 9, 16A to
   18, and 22 to 24) are unexplained by this instrument. My working assumption —
   unverified, since I haven't opened the Rules themselves — is that the skipped
   clauses hold the division headings or introductory provisions in the Rules.
4. **The recurring demands.** Reading all 66, the indicators keep asking for the
   same seven things: a policy or documented process; communication in language the
   person understands; consent; trained workers with records; documented plans that
   get reviewed; records and registers of what happened; and compliance with a named
   companion rulebook (the incident, complaints, restrictive practices or SDA
   conditions rules). Any provider document suite — and any compliance graph — can
   be organised around those seven demand types.

## What is verified and what is not

- **Verified:** every pairing in every table above, every standard name, and the
  authority and compilation details — all taken directly from the instrument text
  fetched from the Federal Register of Legislation on 6 July 2026.
- **My contribution:** the plain-English column and the "seven recurring demands"
  observation are faithful paraphrases and analysis of the indicator text, not
  quotes from the law.
- **Not covered:** the supported independent living standards added in 2026 (this
  compilation predates them), and the Rules-side clause *titles* (the standard
  names here come from the Guidelines' section headings, which may differ slightly
  in wording from the Rules — one known example: the Guidelines say "Enrolment of
  SDA Properties" where other sources say "dwellings").
