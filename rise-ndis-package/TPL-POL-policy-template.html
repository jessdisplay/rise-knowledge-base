3. Purpose

[2–4 short sentences. What this policy achieves for participants and the organisation. No legal citations here — they live in §2.]

Example: This policy makes sure every incident involving a participant is identified, responded to, recorded, and learned from. It protects participants first, and it makes sure we meet our legal duty to notify the NDIS Commission about reportable incidents.

4. Scope

5. Policy statements

[The commitments. Numbered, one idea per statement, ≤ 20 words each where possible, "We will / We do not" voice. These are the citable units — an auditor or a worker can point to "POL-INC-01, statement 3".]

Example set:

  1. The safety and wellbeing of participants comes before every other consideration when an incident occurs.
  2. We record every incident, including near misses, in the Incident Register.
  3. We notify the NDIS Commission of reportable incidents within the timeframes set by the Incident Rules, using our Reportable Incidents Notification Procedure.
  4. We support participants affected by an incident, including access to advocates and the information they need in a way they understand.
  5. We review incidents to find causes and we act on what we learn.
  6. We never punish anyone for raising an incident in good faith.

6. What this means for participants (optional box, participant voice)

[One short paragraph in first person, mirroring the participant-statement style the new SIL Practice Standards use. Recommended for participant-facing topics.]

Example: If something goes wrong, the people supporting me act quickly to keep me safe. They tell me what happened, listen to me, and involve the people I choose. I will not be treated worse for speaking up.
