You own getting Rise OS into production with a real provider's real data in it. This brief is self-contained: where the product is, the plan it sits inside, and exactly what to build. Every number is measured from the repositories at dev or counted on disk.

01Context

Where the product actually is

Shared context, so this brief stands on its own.

Rise OS is real and running on staging, not a prototype. Sixteen route groups, ten front end feature modules, thirteen back end modules, 62 migrations. Auth with mandatory 2FA and passkeys, organisations and tenancy, the legislation model with the NDIS Practice Standards seeded, the obligations engine, expected documents, a Tiptap editor with version history and real time collaboration, anchored comments, and the auditor workspace with cross-tenant grants, submission packs and findings.

Production does not exist yet, and the blocker is three GCP account actions only Jesse can perform. That is his item and it is first in the plan.

Zain is on the compliance engine for the same period: registers, improvement actions, and whether document relationships should be typed rather than inferred. His work lands in the schema, so expect migrations from him during Cycle 2.

One open question sits above this brief: whether Rise serves providers, auditors, or both. It is genuinely unresolved. Nothing in this brief depends on how it resolves. Do not wait on it.

The _Rise_/ folder already carries HCS-REG-DOC-001_Master-Document-Register and splits the suite into Main Policies and Procedures plus Forms, NDIS Module 1 High Intensity Daily Personal Activities, NDIS Module 2A Implementing Behaviour Support, Organisational Governance and Business Management, and Support Coordinator. That grouping is a useful shape to import against.

For the target model these have to resolve into, the reference suite is on this same site: the 123-document suite index, and all 70 standards with their implementing documents. You do not need to understand the whole model, that is Zain's job, but the import has to land against it.

Two constraints on this data

Organisational data only. Policies, procedures, forms, registers and audit evidence. No participant records, so nothing here waits on privacy work and there is no personal or health information in scope.

In Drive, **Rise** is the only writable folder. The other folders in that audit share are the provider's own source files. Read them, never write to them.

I am not asking for a coverage percentage. The narrower question: anywhere an auditor's conclusion depends on our logic, is that logic covered. The obligations engine is already that question answered properly. Use it as the reference rather than a target number.

One end-to-end spec means almost no automated proof that a whole journey works. Before we put a real provider in front of a real auditor, the submission-pack journey should be covered end to end.
