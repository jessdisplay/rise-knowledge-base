01The answer

Where we actually are

Rise OS is a real two-sided compliance platform running on staging, not a prototype. The provider side and the auditor side both exist, the obligations engine already encodes actual NDIS legislation, and the database enforces tenant isolation at the row level.

It is not in production, and the thing blocking production is not engineering. It is three one-time GCP account actions that only an org owner can perform. That is you.

The gap between "what is built" and "what customers can use" is therefore much smaller than it feels from the outside, and it is closed by an afternoon of account administration plus a defined scope of hardening, not by months of new development.

The finding that matters most

The compliance engine is not a greenfield project. It already exists. obligations.service.ts is 494 lines with a 758-line test spec, and it encodes real statute: the s 20(4) to (5) early-childhood carve-down and the s 22 government Core Module floor from the NDIS Provider Registration and Practice Standards Rules 2018. Three ADRs (0010, 0012, 0013) record why those entity rules live in engine code rather than seed data, after the data-driven version was built and deliberately reversed.

This changes Zain's brief entirely. He is not being asked to invent a compliance engine. He is being asked to take a working one to the depth the dossier describes.

03Deployment

Staging is live. Production is waiting on you.

Staging runs on Cloud Run behind a Google IAP gate at *.staging.riseos.care, with Cloud SQL over private IP, Secret Manager, an external HTTPS load balancer and an assets CDN. Eight of the nine infrastructure milestones are complete. CI, security scanning, PR convention checks and a deploy-cloud-run-prod.yml workflow are all already written.

Production infrastructure is coded and gated behind one variable. Applying it needs three actions that no engineer on the team can perform:

  1. Create the riseos-prod GCP project.
  2. Link billing to it.
  3. Grant dev@ Owner on it.

The full sequence is written up in riseOS-infra/docs/prod-bringup.md. Until those three things happen, "production ready ASAP" has a hard ceiling that no amount of engineering pressure will lift.

  • The prod project stood up. Three account actions, yours, blocking everything else.

06Working

Four things to change about how we work

Kept deliberately few. Each one exists because of something in this document, not because it is good practice in the abstract.

One name against every piece of work

Not a team, not a pair. One person owns the outcome and makes the call. Right now the ownership is implicit, which is why April has ended up carrying the infrastructure alone and why the prod blocker has sat unresolved without anyone's name on it. Zain owns the compliance engine. April owns production readiness. I own the prod project bringup, and it goes on the board like anything else.

Decisions get written down before they get made

A short piece of writing first: what we are doing, what could go wrong, what we are assuming, how we will know it worked. The team already does this well in the ADRs, and ADR 0012 is the proof, it records a decision being built, tested against reality and reversed, with the reasoning intact. That habit should reach past the database layer to everything that matters.

One roadmap, scoped so it can finish

One ordered list for the product, not one per person. Work gets cut down until it can actually land, and blockers beat new surface. There is no such list today: GitHub carries zero open issues, the real backlog sits in Linear where I have not been looking, and I did not know the state of my own product until it was read back to me out of the code. That is the gap this closes.

Ship continuously, in small pieces

To staging always, to production in increments once it exists. Back end commits went 116 in July to 40 in August, and nothing since the 18th. That is what work accumulating without a release to aim at looks like.
