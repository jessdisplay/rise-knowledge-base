You are here

First: the rule

Someone wrote down what good work looks like.

1 · The standard

A standard is a rule about doing good work.

The government writes the standards.

Each standard says what good looks like for one part of your work.

Hard word: standard. A standard is a written rule. It says what good work looks like.

Example: one standard says people's money must be kept safe.

Level 2: which rules are yours?See every standard in plain words

Then: you do the work

Your organisation turns the rule into everyday actions.

2 · The policy

A policy says what we promise to do.

Your organisation writes a policy for each rule.

The policy is your promise about how you will follow that rule.

Hard word: policy. A policy is a short document. It says what your organisation promises to do.

Example: "We keep people's money safe. We only spend it the way they ask."

See a real-shaped policy
3 · The procedure

A procedure shows how we do it, step by step.

A promise is not enough.

The procedure tells workers exactly what to do, in order.

Hard word: procedure. A procedure is a list of steps. It shows how to do the work the right way.

Example: "Step 1: count the money together. Step 2: write it down. Step 3: both sign."

See how a procedure connects
4 · The form

A form is what we fill in when we do the work.

Forms catch the facts while the work happens.

Who, what, when — written down straight away.

Hard word: form. A form is a page with spaces to fill in. It records what you did.

Example: writing down that Sam got his medicine at 8 o'clock.

See a form in the chain

Then: you keep proof

The records show the work really happened.

5 · The register

A register is the list that keeps every record together.

One form is one moment.

The register collects them all, so nothing gets lost.

Hard word: register. A register is like a logbook. Every finished form goes into it.

Example: the medicine register shows every dose, every day, for everyone.

See a register with rows
6 · The evidence

Evidence is proof that the work really happened.

Evidence is anything that proves it: signed forms, register rows, training records.

No proof means no one can tell good work from good intentions.

Hard word: evidence. Evidence means proof. It shows the promise was kept.

Example: the signed form is proof Sam got his medicine.

Follow proof through the chain

Then: someone checks

A person from outside looks at the proof.

7 · The audit

An audit is when someone outside checks our proof.

The auditor does not work for you.

They read your documents, talk to your people, and look at real records.

Hard word: audit. An audit is a careful check by an outside person. They test if the promise is real.

Example: the auditor opens the medicine register and picks three days to check.

See the whole audit journey
8 · The finding

A finding is what the checker tells us to fix.

A finding is not a punishment.

It points at a gap between the promise and the proof.

Hard word: finding. A finding is the auditor's note about something that needs fixing.

Example: "Two days in the register have no signature."

See how findings work
9 · The improvement

An improvement is how we fix it and get better.

You fix the gap and change the documents so it cannot happen again.

Then the circle starts again at step 2 — better than before.

Hard word: improvement. An improvement is the fix. It closes the gap the finding pointed at.

Example: a new rule — no register page is closed without two signatures.
