# Rise Zero-Knowledge Training Model — v1.0 (confirmed)

**Status:** confirmed by Rise Development, 7 July 2026 — (a) the nine-step referent
stands as drafted in §3 L1 (*standard → policy → procedure → form → register →
evidence → audit → finding → improvement action*), and (b) depth is open with guided
defaults per §4.4: progress is celebrated, nothing is locked. This document now
governs the build. Sources: §8.

---

## 1. The reframe (Rise Development's instruction, distilled)

The primary user is not the auditor. It is the provider's own people — support
workers, coordinators, managers, boards — who are not compliance-trained and mostly
do not understand the framework they are audited against. Motivation is structural,
not marginal: around 44% of Australian adults read at or below a year-10 literacy
level (PIAAC, as cited by Australian government accessibility guidance), so the
standard instruments are inaccessible to a large share of the workforce itself.

Therefore: **Rise is a training tool first.** Using it teaches compliance; it never
assumes it. The governing law: *when we don't understand, we are all infants* —
build assuming zero knowledge, and let depth educate. **Depth is the curriculum**:
the top of the graph is the simplest complete picture, and every level down adds
exactly one kind of new complexity. Descending the graph and learning the subject
are the same movement.

## 2. What the great indexes teach (the rocks, each with its lesson)

| Index | Mechanism | Rise translation | Standing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Genome browsers (UCSC, Ensembl) | One dataset, continuous zoom from chromosome to base pair — magnification changes, the database never does | One verified graph, many zoom surfaces; never a second "simple" database that can drift | Established |
| Beck's Underground map (1933) and cartographic generalisation | Deliberately distort geometry to gain comprehension; preserve topology; each zoom shows only what that zoom needs | Simplified views may drop detail but may never alter a connection; every simplification links to its full version | Established |
| Anatomical atlases | Same body, peeled one layer at a time | Layer-peeling = the drill-down we already built | Established |
| The periodic table | Position encodes meaning; the beginner and the expert read the *same grid* at different depths | Address stamps and the colour language carry meaning at every level; one artefact serves all readers | Established |
| Library classification | Hierarchical addresses let a stranger find anything without knowing the subject | The dual statutory address is a call number | Established |
| Simple English Wikipedia | A parallel corpus — same topics, simpler prose, linked both ways, never replacing the full article | Per-node parallel renderings, always linked to the authoritative text | Established |
| Game onboarding | Teach one mechanic at a time, in context, at the moment of need; reveal complexity as competence grows; always show where you are | The nine-step flow is the tutorial level; every node teaches its own next layer; persistent "you are here" | Industry consensus (design practice) |
| Progressive disclosure | Show the primary thing; defer the secondary until asked | Default-shallow rendering with "go deeper" on every element | Established UX principle |
| Scaffolding / zone of proximal development (Vygotsky) | The next step should sit just beyond current comfort, with support | Levels differ by exactly one kind of complexity; the system offers the next layer, never the whole stack | Established educational theory |
| AAC boards and Australian Easy Read practice | Consistent symbol-and-position language; one idea per sentence; an image per concept; ≥14pt sans-serif; white space; explain every hard word; co-design and user-test with people with intellectual disability | The Easy Read layer per node, built to Australian conventions, with the stable colour/position language we already use everywhere | Verified guidance (Style Manual; Inclusion Australia; NSW/SA toolkits) — see §6 evidence caveat |

## 3. The level architecture

- **L0 — Four boxes.** *The rule says what good is → you do the work → you keep
  proof → someone checks.* Everyone's first screen; already built (atlas figure 4).
- **L1 — The nine-step journey (the tutorial level and the permanent home map).**
  Draft referent, awaiting confirmation: *standard → policy → procedure → form →
  register → evidence → audit → finding → improvement action.* One plain sentence
  and one image slot per step, Easy Read discipline throughout.
- **L2 — Your world.** Modules pruned to the learner's provider profile using the
  Rules' s 20(3) registration table (verified, legislated). A learner only ever
  descends into standards that apply to them.
- **L3 — The standards in plain English.** The existing 66-standard plain-English
  map is this layer's content, node by node.
- **L4 — The golden threads.** Indicator ↔ the organisation's actual documents,
  with the gap engine's states in human words: *we say it / we do it / we can prove
  it* — and the two failure kinds as *nothing says it* and *paper only*.
- **L5 — The letter of the law.** Addresses, dual titles, the ingested authorised
  text, and the auditor's test of one indicator.
- **L6 — The graph itself.** Edges, versions, bitemporal history — the builder's
  and expert auditor's layer.

**Binding rules:** each level adds exactly one kind of new complexity; every claim
at level *n* links to its substantiation at level *n+1*; the statutory address is
never more than one tap away from anything, at any level.

## 4. Design laws

1. **One graph, many surfaces.** No second simplified dataset, ever — simplified
   copies drift, and drift in compliance is harm.
2. **Distort to teach, never to lie.** Simplification may omit; it may not alter a
   connection. Every simplified statement carries its full-version link.
3. **Same language everywhere.** The colour and position code (purple law, teal
   framework, coral provider, pink check; address stamps) is the product's AAC
   board — identical at every level, learnable once.
4. **Open depth, guided defaults — recommendation, decision pending.** Games gate
   progression; accessibility practice and the rights framing (timely access, no
   added cost) argue against ever locking public legal information away from adult
   users. Recommended: default each user to their level, celebrate progression, but
   never lock deeper layers. Trade-off stated honestly: gating gives cleaner pacing
   and a stronger sense of achievement; locking is simpler to build; open depth
   risks overwhelm, mitigated by defaults and "you are here." Alternative if pacing
   is wanted: progress badges without locks.
5. **Teach at the moment of need.** Every hard word explains itself where it
   appears (the sector's own Easy Read example is explaining the word
   "legislation"). No glossary-only knowledge.
6. **Co-design or it doesn't count.** The Easy Read layer is built *with* people
   with intellectual disability and communication support needs, engages Easy Read
   expertise as the Style Manual directs, and is comprehension-tested with the
   pilot provider's actual staff. See §6.
7. **Plain never means vague.** Every plain sentence remains audit-traceable to its
   address — layered access to the same truth, nothing dumbed down, everything
   reachable.

## 6. Honest limits (the load-bearing caveats)

1. **The Easy Read evidence gap.** Peer-reviewed evidence on Easy Read's
   comprehension effectiveness is limited and mixed: a 2017 meta-narrative review
   found no clear evidence of improved understanding from Easy Read health
   information as public information; a 2017 comparative study found Easy Read did
   not outperform standard text even with support; practices vary and no
   accreditation exists. Rise therefore treats the accessible layer as an equity
   obligation and established government practice *and* as a hypothesis whose
   benefit for Rise's users must be validated in the pilot. Design law 6 is a build
   requirement, not a nicety.
2. **The 44% literacy figure** is PIAAC-derived and quoted by government
   accessibility sources — used here as motivation, not as a precise measurement of
   the NDIS workforce.
3. **Two decisions are Rise Development's, not mine:** the nine-step referent (L1)
   and gating vs open depth (§4.4).
4. **Expertise boundary:** I am not an Easy Read practitioner, and the Style Manual
   itself says to engage one. Budget for it; co-design credits belong to the people
   who do that work.
5. Training-tool framing does not guarantee training outcomes — comprehension and
   behaviour change need measuring in the pilot, the same "coverage is not
   adequacy" humility the gap engine carries.
