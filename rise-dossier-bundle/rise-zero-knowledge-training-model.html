Rise Zero-Knowledge Training Model — v1.0 (confirmed)

Status: confirmed by Rise Development, 7 July 2026 — (a) the nine-step referent stands as drafted in §3 L1 (standard → policy → procedure → form → register → evidence → audit → finding → improvement action), and (b) depth is open with guided defaults per §4.4: progress is celebrated, nothing is locked. This document now governs the build. Sources: §8.

1. The reframe (Rise Development's instruction, distilled)

The primary user is not the auditor. It is the provider's own people — support workers, coordinators, managers, boards — who are not compliance-trained and mostly do not understand the framework they are audited against. Motivation is structural, not marginal: around 44% of Australian adults read at or below a year-10 literacy level (PIAAC, as cited by Australian government accessibility guidance), so the standard instruments are inaccessible to a large share of the workforce itself.

Therefore: Rise is a training tool first. Using it teaches compliance; it never assumes it. The governing law: when we don't understand, we are all infants — build assuming zero knowledge, and let depth educate. Depth is the curriculum: the top of the graph is the simplest complete picture, and every level down adds exactly one kind of new complexity. Descending the graph and learning the subject are the same movement.

3. The level architecture

  • L0 — Four boxes. The rule says what good is → you do the work → you keep proof → someone checks. Everyone's first screen; already built (atlas figure 4).
  • L1 — The nine-step journey (the tutorial level and the permanent home map). Draft referent, awaiting confirmation: standard → policy → procedure → form → register → evidence → audit → finding → improvement action. One plain sentence and one image slot per step, Easy Read discipline throughout.
  • L2 — Your world. Modules pruned to the learner's provider profile using the Rules' s 20(3) registration table (verified, legislated). A learner only ever descends into standards that apply to them.
  • L3 — The standards in plain English. The existing 66-standard plain-English map is this layer's content, node by node.
  • L4 — The golden threads. Indicator ↔ the organisation's actual documents, with the gap engine's states in human words: we say it / we do it / we can prove it — and the two failure kinds as nothing says it and paper only.
  • L5 — The letter of the law. Addresses, dual titles, the ingested authorised text, and the auditor's test of one indicator.
  • L6 — The graph itself. Edges, versions, bitemporal history — the builder's and expert auditor's layer.

Binding rules: each level adds exactly one kind of new complexity; every claim at level n links to its substantiation at level n+1; the statutory address is never more than one tap away from anything, at any level.

4. Design laws

  1. One graph, many surfaces. No second simplified dataset, ever — simplified copies drift, and drift in compliance is harm.
  2. Distort to teach, never to lie. Simplification may omit; it may not alter a connection. Every simplified statement carries its full-version link.
  3. Same language everywhere. The colour and position code (purple law, teal framework, coral provider, pink check; address stamps) is the product's AAC board — identical at every level, learnable once.
  4. Open depth, guided defaults — recommendation, decision pending. Games gate progression; accessibility practice and the rights framing (timely access, no added cost) argue against ever locking public legal information away from adult users. Recommended: default each user to their level, celebrate progression, but never lock deeper layers. Trade-off stated honestly: gating gives cleaner pacing and a stronger sense of achievement; locking is simpler to build; open depth risks overwhelm, mitigated by defaults and "you are here." Alternative if pacing is wanted: progress badges without locks.
  5. Teach at the moment of need. Every hard word explains itself where it appears (the sector's own Easy Read example is explaining the word "legislation"). No glossary-only knowledge.
  6. Co-design or it doesn't count. The Easy Read layer is built with people with intellectual disability and communication support needs, engages Easy Read expertise as the Style Manual directs, and is comprehension-tested with the pilot provider's actual staff. See §6.
  7. Plain never means vague. Every plain sentence remains audit-traceable to its address — layered access to the same truth, nothing dumbed down, everything reachable.

6. Honest limits (the load-bearing caveats)

  1. The Easy Read evidence gap. Peer-reviewed evidence on Easy Read's comprehension effectiveness is limited and mixed: a 2017 meta-narrative review found no clear evidence of improved understanding from Easy Read health information as public information; a 2017 comparative study found Easy Read did not outperform standard text even with support; practices vary and no accreditation exists. Rise therefore treats the accessible layer as an equity obligation and established government practice and as a hypothesis whose benefit for Rise's users must be validated in the pilot. Design law 6 is a build requirement, not a nicety.
  2. The 44% literacy figure is PIAAC-derived and quoted by government accessibility sources — used here as motivation, not as a precise measurement of the NDIS workforce.
  3. Two decisions are Rise Development's, not mine: the nine-step referent (L1) and gating vs open depth (§4.4).
  4. Expertise boundary: I am not an Easy Read practitioner, and the Style Manual itself says to engage one. Budget for it; co-design credits belong to the people who do that work.
  5. Training-tool framing does not guarantee training outcomes — comprehension and behaviour change need measuring in the pilot, the same "coverage is not adequacy" humility the gap engine carries.

7. Proposed next artefact

A working L0→L1 prototype: the nine-step home screen, one plain sentence and one image slot per step, each step opening into the existing artefacts as its deeper layers — the training tool's front door. Built immediately after the two pending confirmations.
