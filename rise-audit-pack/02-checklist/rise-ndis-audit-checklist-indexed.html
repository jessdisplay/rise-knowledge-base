The indexing scheme (as recovered)

  • Rise standard ID = MODULE-ordinal. Core is sub-scored by division: CORE-<division>.<ordinal> (e.g. CORE-4.4 = Division 4 "support environment", 4th standard = Mealtime management). Modules use a flat ordinal: M1-3, M2-2, M5-4, VER-1. [V-prior] for Core/M1/M2 (seen in the register); [D] for the ordinal numbering of M4/M5/VER (I ordered them by QI section — reconcile against the register).
  • Dual address = Rules address (which Schedule + clause of the Provider Registration and Practice Standards Rules 2018, F2018L00631) + Indicators address (which section of the Quality Indicators Guidelines, base F2018N00041 → current F2026C00528). [V-prior]
  • Module → Schedule map [V-prior]: Core → Sch 1 (QI ss 6–27) · M1 → Sch 2 (ss 29–35) · M2 → Sch 3 (ss 38–44) · M2A → Sch 4 (schedule no. inferred [D]) (ss 47–54) · M3 → Sch 5 (ss 56–62) · M4 → Sch 6 (ss 64–66) · M5 → Sch 7 (ss 68–72) · SIL/M5A → QI ss 72B–72E (Rules schedule/clauses [GAP]) · Verification → Sch 8 (ss 74–77, reverse-paired).

How to read a row: Rise ID · Standard name · Rules @ · QI @ · what the auditor tests + evidence to have ready. The auditor's universal method is unchanged from the guide: say it (policy) → do it (procedure + form) → prove it (records + interview + observation), triangulated. [V-now]

10. Cross-cutting evidence-hygiene layer (applies to every standard)

The "seven recurring demands" your prior read of all 66 standards surfaced — the indicators keep asking for the same seven things [V-prior]: 1. a policy or documented process; 2. communication in language the person understands; 3. consent; 4. trained workers with records; 5. documented plans that get reviewed; 6. records/registers of what happened; 7. compliance with a named companion rulebook (Incident, Complaints, Restrictive Practices, or SDA Conditions rules).

Cross-cutting auditor behaviours to design one-click graph queries for [V-now / C]: - registers before prose; dates compared (screening vs start date; incident vs entry; notification vs deadline); - version footers checked against the master register; - workers asked to describe a procedure, answer compared to the document; - participants interviewed with opt-out sampling (Commission-verified); - the evidence trail walked in both directions (policy→evidence and evidence→policy).

Evidence-hygiene checklist (every document): version-numbered + dated · policy has matching procedure + form/register · forms have completed instances on file · registers are live · legislative references current · staff can articulate it · participants confirm it · a policy-to-standard map exists and is current · self-assessment done against the current standards · everything scaled to size, scale, scope and complexity. [D, distilled from the real report]
