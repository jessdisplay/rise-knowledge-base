01Direction

What we are building

Rise OS is the audit platform for both sides of the table. Providers keep their compliance in it. Auditors work in the same system, against the same evidence, with their own access. Everything in it is anchored to the actual legislation rather than to a checklist someone typed up.

That last part is the moat. The obligations engine already derives a provider's real duties from their entity type and registration classes, including the s 20(4) to (5) early-childhood carve-down and the s 22 government Core Module floor. Nobody else in this market does that from the instrument. Everything we build should make that chain more complete and more provable, not add surface beside it.

The next milestone is singular: a real provider, audited end to end, in production. Harmony Care is that provider. Every cycle below is ordered by what it takes to get there.

02Working

Four things I am changing about how we work

These are on me. I have not given this team enough direction, and the four below are the fix.

One name against every piece of work

Not a team, not a pair. One person owns the outcome and makes the call. Ownership has been implicit until now, which is how April ended up carrying the entire infrastructure alone and how the production blocker sat for weeks with nobody's name against it, including mine.

Decisions get written down before they get made

Short and in prose: what we are doing, what could go wrong, what we are assuming, how we will know it worked. You already do this properly in the ADRs, and 0012 is the best example in the repository, a decision built, tested against reality, reversed, with the reasoning left intact for whoever comes next. I want that habit to reach past the database layer.

One roadmap, scoped so it can finish

One ordered list for the product, not one per person. Work gets cut down until it can actually land, and blockers beat new surface. There has not been such a list, and that is my fault rather than yours: the backlog lives in Linear and I have not been in it, which is how a blocker that only I could clear sat for weeks without anyone being able to point at it.

Ship continuously, in small pieces

To staging always, to production in increments once it exists. Back end commits went from 116 in July to 40 in August and nothing since the 18th. That is what work accumulating without a release to aim at looks like, and it is a planning failure, not an effort one.

04Zain

The compliance engine

Owner: Zain Al-Saffi

Why you. This is the hardest modelling problem in the product and the part competitors cannot copy, and modelling is your strength. Prompt work is not where you should be.

What you are not doing. You are not building a compliance engine from scratch. One exists and it is good: obligations.service.ts, with entity rules that encode real statute, and ADRs 0010, 0012 and 0013 recording why they live in engine code after the data-driven version was built and deliberately reversed. Read those three first. Your job is depth, not replacement.

The gap, precisely

The Rise dossier defines a nine-step chain: standard → policy → procedure → form → register → evidence → audit → finding → improvement action. Against the schema as it stands today, seven of those nine links exist. Two do not:

  • Register. There is no registers table. Registers are how a provider proves a control actually operates rather than merely existing, and an auditor asks for them by name.
  • Improvement action. Findings carry severity, due date, rationale and conformance, but nothing carries a finding forward into a corrective action that can be tracked to closure. Right now the chain stops at the diagnosis.
The most interesting problem in the product

ADR 0011 chose flat node linking with inferred document relationships. The dossier defines nine typed relationships, EXISTS TO MEET, IMPLEMENTS, OPERATIONALISES, REFERENCES, USES, RECORDS TO, GENERATED BY, EVIDENCES, ASSESSES, and it distinguishes claims that are Verified, Confirmed, Tested or Draft.

Inference gets you a link. It does not get you a defensible reason for the link, and a defensible reason is the entire product. Whether ADR 0011 should now be superseded is your call to make and to write up. If it should stand, say why and we keep it.

Your substrate

Everything you need is already assembled and does not need rebuilding:

  • The all-in-one dossier: the collection, everything in one document , the 123-document suite, the graph, the training ladder, the auditor's view.
  • The knowledge base: rise-knowledge-base , the audit pack, the standards navigator, the document register.

First deliverable

Not code. An ADR that maps our current chain against the dossier's, states which of the two missing links we build first and why, and takes a position on typed relationships versus inference. Written before anything is built, so we can argue with the reasoning rather than with the migration.

05April

Production readiness

Owner: April Kidd

Why you. You built the infrastructure, you are the only person who has been near it, and you wrote the prod bringup procedure. Nobody else can own this and it should have had your name on it formally long before now.

The honest part. You have been carrying infra on your own alongside the heaviest feature load on the team. That is not sustainable and it is not a compliment, it is a risk. Part of this brief is telling me what to take off you.

What you own

  • Production. Apply prod the moment I clear the account blocker, then rehearse the promotion path rather than trusting it on the day.
  • Harmony Care as the first real tenant. Their organisational compliance suite loaded and resolving correctly against their obligations. Organisational data only.
  • Test depth where it counts. 24 back end specs, 34 front end tests and one end-to-end spec is thin for software an auditor relies on. I am not asking for a coverage number. I am asking that anywhere an auditor's conclusion depends on our logic, that logic is covered. The obligations engine is the standard to match.

Not on this list: Participants. We are not working with participant data yet, so it stays scaffolded and nobody should spend a cycle on it. If that changes it becomes its own project, not a line item here.

What I need back from you

  1. What actually changed in August. Velocity halved and I do not know why. If it is something I did, or did not do, I would rather hear it straight.
  2. What comes off your plate so infra is not a single point of failure.
  3. Your read on whether Cycle 1 is real in two weeks. If it is not, say so now and we change the plan rather than the deadline.
